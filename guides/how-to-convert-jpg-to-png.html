How to Convert JPG to PNG

Last updated: January 2026

JPG and PNG are the most common image formats on the internet, and they exist for very different reasons. JPG keeps file sizes small by throwing away some image data. PNG keeps every pixel intact but produces larger files. If you need to convert a JPG to PNG, this guide will explain when it makes sense, how to do it, and what to watch out for.

Why Convert JPG to PNG?

The main reason people convert JPG to PNG is transparency. JPG does not support transparent backgrounds — every JPG is a solid rectangle. But PNG does. If you've ever placed a JPG logo on a colored background and seen a white box appear around it, that's the missing transparency PNG would have given you.

The second reason is lossless editing. JPG is a lossy format, which means every time you edit and save it, it loses some quality. PNG is lossless, so you can edit and save it as many times as you want without losing quality. If you plan to make a lot of edits to an image, converting it to PNG first can help preserve its quality.

When You Should NOT Convert JPG to PNG

PNG files are often five to ten times larger than the JPG they came from. If you're converting a photo just to have a PNG version, you're making the file bigger for no benefit. Photos don't need transparency, and they don't benefit much from lossless storage.

If your goal is a smaller file, you want the opposite direction — convert PNG to JPG, or convert either one to WebP. If your goal is a sharper photo, PNG won't help. Once JPG compression has thrown away detail, converting to PNG doesn't bring it back. It just locks in what's already there.

What Happens to the Image Quality?

Converting JPG to PNG does not improve quality. It preserves whatever quality the JPG had at that moment. If the JPG was already compressed and slightly blurry, the PNG will be slightly blurry too — just without any further loss from future edits.

Think of it this way: JPG is like a photocopy that fades each time you copy it. PNG is like a digital scan that stays the same forever. But neither one can restore detail that was already gone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is converting a low-quality JPG to PNG and expecting the result to look better. It won't. The quality was lost the moment the original JPG was compressed.

The second mistake is using PNG for photos that will be uploaded to a website. A single high-resolution PNG photo can easily be 5 MB or more. That slows down page load times and hurts your site's performance in search rankings. For web photos, WebP or JPG is almost always the better choice.

PNG vs WebP

WebP is a newer format that supports transparency like PNG but produces much smaller files. If you're converting for a website and don't need maximum compatibility, WebP is usually the better output format.

The reason to choose PNG over WebP is compatibility. PNG works everywhere — old software, old browsers, old operating systems. WebP is supported by all modern browsers, but some older tools still can't open it. If you're sending a file to someone who might have an old system, PNG is the safer choice.

Summary

Convert JPG to PNG when you need transparency, when you plan to edit the image many times, or when you need maximum compatibility. Don't convert when your only goal is a smaller file or a sharper photo — PNG won't give you either.
